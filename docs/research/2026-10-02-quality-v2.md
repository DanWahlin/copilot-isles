# Quality v2: Tidewater's look by default, one game that scales down (2026-10-02)

> Revises [the first quality-bar note](2026-10-02-quality-bar.md) after a deeper pass. Tidewater's three.js
> build was run and timed on Dan's M3 Max beside Copilot Isles, both renderers were mapped file by file, and
> the characters were judged against Disney's principles of animation.

**Dan's asks:**

1. Make Tidewater's quality the default.
2. Degrade gracefully on low-power devices from **one** version: never load two versions, never maintain two.
3. Hold the characters to the same bar: what would Walt and his animators call high quality?

## Answers

1. **Tidewater's look runs in three.js.** Its three.js build (`dgreenheck/tidewater@fd6cae6`, MIT) used
   three.js **0.186, the same release as ours**, and it already had the look: FFT ocean, physical sky with
   volumetric clouds, TAAU, GTAO and motion blur. On the M3 Max it ran about as fast as our game does today
   (§1). Its author moved to raw WebGPU a day later for more headroom. We don't need to: we adopt its methods
   inside our budgets, mostly through three.js's own post nodes.
2. **One version can scale down.** Every effect has one implementation with quality settings and one cheap
   state. The tier is chosen before anything heavy loads, and the build derives the smaller assets from one
   master (§3).
3. **Tidewater isn't the bar for characters.** Its three.js build has no people at all. The later build adds
   two Microsoft Rocketbox vendors with no facial animation, blinks or lip sync. Walt's bar is life and
   appeal: eyes, faces, acting, secondary motion and staging. That's our biggest gap, and it costs almost no
   GPU time, so it ships on every tier (§4).

## 2. Where Tidewater's look comes from, ranked by look per millisecond

Costs are relative. The slice measures real numbers per tier.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

three.js 0.186 already ships `GTAONode`, `TAAUNode`, `TRAANode`, `SSSNode` (contact shadows), `BloomNode`,

`CSMShadowNode`. The custom work is the atmosphere, the clouds, the ground bounce, the auto exposure and the
surfaces, all adapted from Tidewater's MIT TSL with its header.

## 3. One version that scales down

1. **One implementation per effect.** Each effect reads its settings from the tier table
   (`src/engine/quality.ts`, already the single source). It has a full state and one cheap state, and the
   cheap state is "baked by the same code" or "off". There's never a second copy of a shader:
   - Volumetric clouds → a sky dome baked at load from the same cloud shader.
   - Dynamic sky → time-of-day keys baked from the same atmosphere code and blended.
   - GTAO → the AO we already bake into terrain and props.
   - TAAU → FXAA at the native scale.
   - Contact shadows, flare, motion blur → off.
2. **Pick the tier, then load.** During the title, a capability check (WebGPU or WebGL2, compute, limits)
   and a short GPU probe pick the tier. Only that tier's packs download. Code that only High and Ultra use
   (clouds, GTAO, TAAU) loads through dynamic `import()`, so a Chromebook never fetches it.
3. **Author once; the build derives the rest.** Each texture and mesh is made once at full quality. The
   asset build writes KTX2 copies at 2048, 1024 and 512, and meshoptimizer LODs, listed per tier in the pack
   manifest (PLAN §13). Nobody edits a low copy.
4. **Hold the frame rate.** Lower the dynamic resolution first (TAAU hides it on High), then drop one effect
   at a time. Settings can override the tier.
5. **Keep the look contract on every tier.** The palette, filmic curve and grade, sky colours, haze, time
   of day, character faces and acting, and staging are the same on Lite and on Ultra. Only cost scales, so
   every tier is recognisably the same game.

| | Lite (Chromebooks, 30 fps) | Low (UHD 620, 30 fps) | Medium (Iris Xe, M1 Air, 45–60) | High (M2/M3 Air, RTX 3050, 60 @1080p) | Ultra (M Pro/Max, RTX 3060+, 60 @1440p+) |

| Filmic grade, atmosphere, haze | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes |

## 4. Characters: Walt's bar

The twelve principles (Thomas and Johnston, _The Illusion of Life_), with Disney's acting rule that the eyes
lead everything:

| Principle | What it means for us | How | Tiers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Appeal | Each teacher reads at a glance: silhouette, proportion, colour and prop | Bone-scale proportions, costume pieces, signature props, a one-page style guide | all |
| The eyes lead (acting) | Blinks every 2–6 s, sometimes double; eye darts; eyes turn first, then the head, toward whoever speaks; a highlight in each eye | Eye bones or a UV-scrolled eye atlas; gaze targets from the dialogue system | all |
| Timing | Mouths match the words | Azure viseme events saved at build time, if our voices return them (test one line first); Rhubarb Lip Sync (MIT) as the fallback; about 8 mouth shapes | all |
| Anticipation, follow-through and overlap | A small wind-up before a gesture; hats, hair, coats and satchels keep moving after the body stops | Reworked gesture curves; 2–6 CPU spring bones per character | all |
| Slow in and out, arcs | Gestures ease and travel on arcs, not straight lines | Eased curves on arcs; mocap gestures (Quaternius UAL CC0, Rocketbox MIT) retargeted | all |
| Secondary action | Breathing, weight shifts, listening nods, handling props | Layered additive idles | all |
| Exaggeration, squash and stretch | Chibi proportions allow bigger poses and a little squash on landings and emphasis | Scale keys on emphasised words (word timings exist) | all |
| Staging | The speaker is framed and lit; nobody walks across them | Talk-shot rules; a key and rim light in conversations; DOF on the speaker | rim on all, DOF on High+ |
| Solid drawing | No clipping, feet planted, hands on props | Contact-sheet fit checks; leg IK (have) | all |

Almost all of this is CPU or texture work, so every tier gets the same performances. **Wingman** becomes a
rigged parrot (wings, tail, head, beak, eyelids) with head tilts and squash on landing. He's the guide, so
he's a hero character. A new hero cast in one style (routes in the first note, §4.4) comes after the slice
proves the bar.

## 5. Art direction: one decision for Dan

Tidewater is photographic. Our people are chibi pirates and our kits are low-poly. Real light lifts
everything, but faceted palms and toy houses can look cheap under it.

- **A. Real light, stylized people (recommended).** Tidewater's sky, light and surfaces, with appealing
  stylized characters in premium-toy materials and expressive faces. Sea of Thieves is the nearest
  reference: exaggerated pirates on real water under a real sky. Faceted palms give way to Tidewater's
  procedural palms (MIT), and hero spots lose their flat surfaces.
- **B. Realistic people.** Matches the light, but loses appeal and needs a whole new cast.
- **C. Today's toy look, plus light only.** Cheapest, but the style split (#19) stays.

## 7. Risks

- **Pipelines and shader warm-up.** Every pass adds pipelines, and #29 is already over at High. Tidewater
  compiled shaders for about 11 s here. Merge passes, warm them behind the loading card, and count them.
- **Bloom cost.** Our earlier bloom cost 15–27 ms (#40). Rebuild it as a half-resolution chain and measure
  with frame-rate tests.
- **No private three.js internals.** Tidewater patches TSL function caching and calls private PMREM
  methods. We use public APIs (`CSMShadowNode`, `PMREMGenerator.fromScene`) and report bugs upstream.
- **WebGL2.** Compute steps (atmosphere LUTs, auto exposure, the ground-bounce bake) need fragment-pass
  versions, or a bake at load.
- **Main thread.** High's budget is under 4 ms per frame (PLAN §12.1). Measure the CPU cost of each pass.
- **Licences.** Adapted Tidewater files keep the MIT header and `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` entry; Poly Haven
  assets go in `ASSETS.md`; visemes are data we generate.
