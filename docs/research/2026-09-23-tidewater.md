# Reference study: Tidewater (dgreenheck/tidewater, 2026-09-23)

**Repo:** https://github.com/dgreenheck/tidewater (commit `d32799f`) · **Live:** https://dgreenheck.github.io/tidewater/
**What it is:** a real-time tropical island + ocean in three.js **0.186** (the same version as us) with **Vite 8** (same as us). It uses **WebGPURenderer + TSL**, and it's **WebGPU-only** (it throws without `navigator.gpu`). About 57k lines of JS.
**License:** **MIT**, © 2026 DRG Software Solutions LLC. **Unlike our other references, we may reuse its code**, provided we keep the copyright + permission notice (see §6). Its third-party assets keep their own licenses: 31 CC0 Freesound recordings, CC0 Poly Haven debris scans, and OFL fonts loaded from Google Fonts. The whale is generated by its own MIT scripts.
**Method:** four parallel code reviews (ocean; sky/lighting/post; world/wildlife/vegetation; engine/loading/audio/UI), plus a live measurement in Chrome (WebGPU, M3 Max, 1600×900).

## Measured (live demo)

**Bottom line:** it's the best-looking browser island we've seen, built for high-end WebGPU machines. **Its techniques and much of its plain-JS code are gold for us. Its load strategy and hardware floor are the opposite of ours.**

## 1. Time of day, sun & night (the feature you love)
- **A simple, cheap solar model:** `timeOfDay` in hours (default 16.2) → `sunDirectionFromTime()` using latitude 24°, declination 6°, and hour angle `(h−12)·15°`, then **rotated by a `sunAzimuth` setting**. `timeSpeed` advances the clock. It's plain vector math.
- **Night factor** = `smoothstep(0.02, 0.18, −sunY)` style, which drives star intensity, the moon (placed opposite the sun with a minimum elevation), and a **sun → moon key-light handoff** below sunY ≈ −0.07. Sun colour comes from atmospheric transmittance, plus sky irradiance and a night ambient.
- **UI:** a draggable **sun-path dial** (day/night arcs, stars, sun, moon) plus a time strip, time speed, and pause (T).
- **What's cheap about changing time:** the static atmosphere LUTs never recompute. Only the **sky-view LUT (192×108)** updates. The **environment cubemap (128) + PMREM refreshes progressively**: one face or roughness level per frame, only after the sun moves more than 0.004 rad or every 3 s.
- **Night:** window and lantern emissives turn on via the night factor. Lanterns, pier lights, village lights, and the flashlight are packed into **the 8 nearest local lights** (inverse-square, optional spot cone, no shadows).
- **WebGPU needed?** Only for the current LUT/readback code. **The model itself is uniforms + CPU math, so it's trivially portable.**

## 2. Sky, clouds & lighting
- **Atmosphere (Hillaire 2020):** transmittance 256×64 (40 steps), multi-scatter 32×32, sky-view 192×108 (32 steps), plus an irradiance integral read back every 0.25 s. All compute → WebGL2 port as **fragment passes into float targets** (well under 1 ms amortized).
- **Clouds (Nubis-style):** 128³ shape + 64³ detail 3D noise, a 512² weather map, and cirrus fibres. Raymarched at **0.6× resolution, 1 of every 4×4 pixels per frame + temporal reprojection**, with empty-space skipping via a 3D SDF, powder/silver lining, and a **256² cloud-shadow map on the land** (¼ of the rows per frame). **Heavy and compute-bound.** WebGL2 alternative: baked noise + a quarter-res 24-48 step raymarch dome, or layered cloud cards on Low. **Cloud shadows on land are the cheap, high-impact part.**
- **Lighting:** 3 shadow cascades (~10/60/400 m) with **contact-hardening PCSS on the near cascade**, **screen-space contact shadows**, a **ground-bounce** approximation (terrain albedo × sun/cloud shadow), GTAO at half-res, and auto exposure.
- **Post order:** HDR MRT (colour, velocity, water mask) → opaque copy → water/transparents → GTAO → underwater/waterline composite → **TAAU (temporal upscale) + RCAS sharpen** → motion blur → bloom → lens flare (occlusion) → lens droplets → exposure/grade/grain/ACES. Dynamic resolution runs from 0.6-1.0 on a CPU-frame-time EMA (↓ above 1.1 × 16.7 ms, ↑ below 0.78×, steps of 0.05).

## 3. Ocean & shore
- **4-cascade FFT ocean** (256² each, patch sizes 733/157/33.3/7.1 m): finite-depth JONSWAP with TMA correction, wind + swell systems, choppiness, and **persistent Jacobian foam** with decay. Two FFT dispatches + two mip passes per frame, rendered on a **CDLOD geomorphed grid** (32² per node, 8 m leaves, 12 levels).
- **Water queries** use GPU compute + async readback (1-3 frames of latency, with 2 fixed-point iterations to invert displacement). The boat **predicts sample positions forward by the measured latency**.
- **Shading:** sky/env + optional planar + **SSR** reflection, GGX sun, refraction into the opaque copy (Beer-Lambert, ~400 m cap), subsurface/backscatter, "crest escape" where light exits thin breaking crests, and foam from Jacobian + shore + sim + wake.
- **Shore:**
  - **ShoreWaves:** analytic, driven by a **precomputed travel-time field**, so waves **refract around headlands**. Period 9 s, breaking γ 0.78, beach slope, and a swash uprush/backwash split.
  - **Breakers:** crest extraction along beach transects → a ballistic lip ribbon + spray, perfectly synced with the surface.
  - **ShoreSim:** a 768² / 380 m swash field (foam, sand wetness, stranded foam, flow speed) with semi-Lagrangian advection and drying.
  - **Surf audio** is timed to the ShoreWaves crash/wash/backwash at 9 stations near the listener.
- **Boat:**
  - **CPU rigid-body physics at 120 Hz:** waterplane buoyancy patches, damping, added mass, resistance, keel lift and cross-flow drag, thrust, rudder lift, trim, and grounding.
  - **WakeSim:** a 512² moving-window **linear free-surface solver in the spectral domain**, depth-aware, with hull pressure and prop wash. It sleeps after 40 s of calm.
  - **Spray:** a 32k-particle GPU pool with atomics.
- **Underwater:** a split view at the near plane with a **meniscus band**, absorption, HG single scattering, caustic shafts, and **lens droplets** after surfacing.
- **Compute-bound:** FFT, queries, ShoreSim, WakeSim, spray, and caustics. **Portable:** CDLOD, ShoreWaves, SurfFoam, water shading, underwater. FFT and the sims *can* run in WebGL2 as fragment ping-pong passes with float targets, at a cost the floor can't pay.

## 4. World: birds, vegetation, terrain, village, life
- **Birds (why they look so good):**
  - 5 species with distinct silhouettes: gulls, terns, pelicans, frigatebirds, and sanderlings
  - **species-specific staging** instead of boids: gulls perch on rails, huts, beach, and **the boat**, and **flush when you approach or drive off**; terns patrol; pelicans fly in a **coordinated line** and plunge-forage; frigatebirds **circle high with almost no flapping**; sanderlings **chase the swash in flocks**
  - `Flyer` is a **point-mass coordinated-turn model** (bank from turn rate, climb/sink, airspeed, wind drift, flap/glide duty)
  - CPU poses: continuous flap phase, glide articulation, tail spread, head motion, **curved landing paths with legs down**
  - **one instanced draw for every species**
  - *Only the batch/deformation is WebGPU-specific. The flight model, states, and poses are plain JS.*
- **Vegetation:**
  - procedural palms, broadleaf trees, banana plants, ferns, and shrubs (`PlantGeometry`)
  - rule-based **scatter** (height, slope, gullies, exclusions, min distance)
  - **grass in 8 m cells** (384 clumps × 7 blades) with **near/mid/far tiers at 18/46/88 m** and dithered fades
  - **6×6 hemi-octahedral impostors baked at startup** (coverage, normal, AO; colour applied at runtime), with a near→impostor handoff at ~75 m for trees and ~45 m for shrubs, thinning from 140-320 m
  - shared wind (bend, flutter, gust fronts, **wind sheen** on grass), leaf translucency, per-instance colour, and **Bayer-dither LOD fades**
  - *Geometry and scatter are plain JS. Wind, impostors, and fades are shader work with no compute needed.*
- **Terrain:** a CPU procedural heightfield with masks, analytic queries, pad flattening, and a min/max pyramid → CDLOD. The splat covers coral/dry/wet sand, seabed, grass, jungle floor, paths, gullies, and rock. A 512² heightfield sun-shadow map is computed by compute (portable to a CPU bake or CSM).
- **Village & props:** data-driven buildings (dimensions, roof, paint, weathering, porch, shutters). **`GeoBuilder` merges primitives into per-material batches** with `tint` + `vdata` (seed, wear, pattern). **Procedural texture baking** (wood, paint, metal, thatch, stone, rope, net, grime) on the GPU at first use (~50-450 ms, ~43 MB). Materials add **tidal wetness, algae, paint chips, rust, sand dust, and splash dirt**. There's also a procedural pier and ~40 prop types (ropes, bollards, barrels, crates, traps, nets, flags, **laundry**).
- **Boats:** hulls are **lofted from naval lines** (centripetal Catmull-Rom sections from keel to sheer, a raked stem) via `GeoKit.loft`. Materials add waterline scum, antifouling, a boot stripe, and non-skid. **This fits our brigantine exactly.**
- **Life:**
  - **reef:** procedural corals and sponges
  - **fish:** ecology instead of one generic school (boids for silversides and bait, lurking groupers, hovering chromis, gliding rays, cruising turtles, **jumping mullet**), frozen beyond 65 m, drawn within 45 m, with screen-size LOD
  - **crabs:** foraging, freezing, sideways sprints, burrows, and more activity at dusk
  - **whale:** a **route-staged "event"** (breaths, blow, roll, dive, **breach**, slap)
  - *Almost all behaviour is plain JS.*

## 5. Engine, audio & UI
- **Frame:** opaque into MRT (colour/velocity/water mask) → opaque colour/depth copy → water + transparents → post. Half-float targets, reversed-Z.
- **Loading:** synchronous construction of the whole world, then `compileAsync` ×2 and 2 warm frames awaited with `onSubmittedWorkDone()`. **There's no lazy loading**, and hidden effects are force-compiled up front. **Hundreds of TSL pipeline variants** (MRT × shadow × transparency × layers) cause the minute-long first load.
- **`TSLPatches.js`** patches two **private** three 0.186 internals: a per-stage function-node cache, and suppressing previous-frame instance matrices. That's fragile across upgrades.
- **Audio (`SoundScape`):**
  - real **CC0 field recordings** played through HRTF panners, filters, buses, a limiter, and ducking
  - gesture-gated and **lazy-loaded** (boat, underwater, pier, and night beds load on demand)
  - **surf timed to the wave simulation**, **footsteps by surface** (sand, wet sand, wood, water, grass, rock), time-of-day-aware beds, and **voice caps** (7 crashes, 5 washes, and so on)
  - about 5 MB of Ogg
- **UI:** dependency-free DOM with **tabs → folders → controls**: sea state (wind, choppiness, swell, fetch), time/sun dial, clouds, quality preset, render scale, FFT/shadow resolution, lens presets, and "reset to recommended". The **HUD fades on inactivity**, and **photo mode** hides the UI.
- **Tests:** one manual shadow test page. **No automated tests**, and CI only builds and deploys.

## 6. Reuse rules (MIT)
- We may **copy and adapt** Tidewater code. Every adapted file keeps a header (`Adapted from Tidewater © 2026 DRG Software Solutions LLC, MIT, <path>@d32799f`), and `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` carries the **full MIT notice**.
- Its **CC0 audio and Poly Haven scans** can be reused and logged in `ASSETS.md`, with the Freesound author and link carried over from their credits.
- **Don't** reuse `TSLPatches.js` without re-validating it against our three version. **Don't** copy its load strategy.
- The other references (Arkenfall, Sakura Valley, Antikythera) stay **technique-only**.

## 8. The decision this raised: renderer & shader language

> **Decided 2026-09-23 by the user: WebGPU-first (`WebGPURenderer` + TSL, with the automatic WebGL2 backend).** The bake-off's intent survives as the **Phase 0 abort rule** (Phase 0 spec §7a, pivot consensus E1): the title must meet §13.5 time-to-interactive on both backends with ≤ 40 pipelines, and the forced-WebGL2 Low shots must pass *before* any compute work starts.

Our plan says **WebGL2 + GLSL** (PLAN §0). Tidewater changes the trade-off:

| Option | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Stay WebGLRenderer + GLSL** | Proven, mature, fast compiles, widest reach. Everything in the plan works today | **Every Tidewater shader must be hand-ported from TSL to GLSL** (weeks of work, error-prone). No path to its compute features, even on capable GPUs. three.js's new features now land in TSL/WebGPU first |
| **B. WebGPURenderer + TSL, with its automatic WebGL2 backend** | **Reuse Tidewater's TSL shaders nearly as-is**, on *both* backends. One shader language. WebGPU devices can scale up to FFT/sims/volumetric clouds, while WebGL2 devices get the non-compute paths. It's three.js's strategic direction | **Compile time** (Tidewater: 30-60 s on an M3 Max, though we'd have far fewer variants). **WebGL2-backend performance on UHD 620 is unmeasured.** No `onBeforeCompile`; the plan's GLSL-patch techniques become node code. `TSLPatches` fragility. Compute features need explicit WebGL2 fallbacks |
| **C. Decide by measurement (recommended)** | A short, bounded **renderer bake-off** before T2 settles it with data | Costs ~1-2 days |

**Recommended bake-off (Phase 0, new task T1b, before T2):** in our repo, build the same micro-scene both ways: CDLOD water + Tidewater's `WaterMaterial` + `ShoreWaves`, one instanced palm field with impostors, a sky with the sun-path model, and the post basics.
- **B-WebGPU** and **B-forced-WebGL2** (`forceWebGL: true`) vs **A-GLSL** (a hand-port of just the water shading).
- **Measure:** cold and warm compile/time-to-interactive, M3 Max GPU ms at the emulated Low/Medium settings (Phase 0 §5), frame-time variance, bundle size, and porting effort per shader.
- **Pass rule:** choose B if B-forced-WebGL2 is within ~15% of A on the emulated Low budget **and** the title time-to-interactive stays within §13.5. Otherwise choose A and port selectively.
