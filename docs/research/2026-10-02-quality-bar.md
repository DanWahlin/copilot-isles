# Raising the quality bar: Tidewater, revisited (2026-10-02)

> **Superseded for the plan by [quality v2](2026-10-02-quality-v2.md)**, which adds measurements, the
> one-version scaling design and the character bar. Its correction: Tidewater's three.js build
> (`fd6cae6`, three.js 0.186) already had the full look, so its methods adapt to our TSL directly.

**Question (Dan):** How do we make Copilot Isles look like Tidewater? Does Tidewater use real assets or
generated ones? How do we lift everything, including the captains and the NPCs, to a level Walt Disney
would approve?

**Short answer:** Tidewater is a hybrid. Nature is generated by code (ocean, sky, clouds, terrain,
plants, fish, birds, even the whale). The things you see up close are real assets: photoscanned props and
textures from Poly Haven, and two Microsoft Rocketbox people with motion-capture clips. Most of its "wow"
comes from light, atmosphere, post-processing, real surface detail and dense set dressing, not from the
characters. Our biggest gaps are the opposite way round: faces that never move, NPCs that are palette
swaps of three rigs, a thin post chain, flat prototype surfaces, and a split art style.

This updates [the 2026-09-23 study](2026-09-23-tidewater.md), which covered Tidewater's environment
techniques. Two things changed since then: Tidewater dropped three.js for its own WebGPU/WGSL engine, and
it added the Rocketbox vendors.

## 1. How Tidewater gets its look (`dgreenheck/tidewater@4811ba4`, MIT)

| Part | Made how | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Ocean (4-cascade FFT, breaking waves, swash sim, caustics, refraction) | Generated (compute + shaders) | README, `src/ocean/` |
| Sky (Hillaire 2020 atmosphere, volumetric cumulus and cirrus, god rays) | Generated | `src/sky/`, CREDITS techniques table |
| Palms, bananas, monstera, broadleaf trees, shrubs, dune grass | Generated geometry **and** leaf textures, with baked impostors and dithered LOD | `src/world/vegetation/PlantGeometry.js`, `LeafTextures.js`, `Impostors.js` |
| 18 fish species, birds, crabs, humpback whale | Generated (the whale is "an original procedural model, generated by our own scripts", traced from a NOAA illustration) | `src/world/fish/`, `src/world/wildlife/`, `src/world/marine/`, CREDITS |
| Stalls, crates, buckets, lanterns, lifebuoy, tables, shelves, beach debris | **Real assets:** Poly Haven CC0 scans and textures, decimated and downsized by `tools/props/` | CREDITS, `public/models/props/`, `public/models/debris/` |
| Joe and Marta (vendors) | **Real assets:** Microsoft Rocketbox avatars (MIT), ~7.5k triangles, 80-bone skin, 1024² PBR textures, 7 mocap clips (idle ×3, talk ×2, wave, shrug), converted by a scripted Blender retarget (`tools/characters/convert.py`) | `tools/characters/README.md` |
| Audio | **Real assets:** 42 Freesound CC0 field recordings | CREDITS |
| Post | GTAO, TAAU + RCAS sharpen, motion blur, bloom, SMAA/FXAA, lens flare with occlusion, lens droplets, air haze, underwater | `src/post/` |

Size: `public/` is 52 MB (6 GLB, 73 JPG, 43 OGG). Ours is about 60 MB, much of it music.

**What makes the screenshots work:** golden-hour light from a physical sky, warm/cool contrast, soft
contact shadows and ambient occlusion, real bloom and flare, scanned wood and metal with real wear, ropes,
lanterns and debris everywhere the camera looks, and water you can see into. There are no flat-coloured
primitives anywhere in frame.

**Reuse note:** Tidewater's code is still MIT, but it's now raw WGSL on its own engine. Reusing it means
porting to TSL (more work than the 09-23 study assumed). Its offline tools (Blender retarget, props
decimation, texture packing) are engine-independent and can be adapted almost as they are.

## 2. Where Copilot Isles stands

- **Characters are real rigged assets already:** Quaternius Pirate Kit GLBs (`captain`, `anne`, `henry` and
  NPC derivatives), Universal Animation Library locomotion retargeted into `.loco` packs, runtime blending,
  stride sync, leg IK, helm grip and head/neck look-at (`src/world/characters/`).
- **Faces never move.** There's no blinking, no lip sync, no brows and no expressions. Azure is used for
  audio only (`tools/voice/generate.ts`).
- **The cast is palette swaps.** 26 NPC ids share 3 rigs, told apart by atlas recolours and a few code-built
  accessories (#46). Talk gestures are sine-wave envelopes written in code (`gestures.ts`).
- **Wingman is a static mesh** cut out of the captain GLB, moved by a procedural flight state machine.
- **Styles are split (#19, Walt):** chibi pirates, half-timbered Medieval Village MegaKit houses, and a
  realistic ocean and sky.
- **Post is thin:** neutral tone mapping, vibrance/contrast/vignette, a bright-pixel "bloom" lift, FXAA and
  dithering (`src/engine/post.ts`). No ambient occlusion (#12), no real bloom or SMAA (#40), no TAA, no LUT,
  no depth of field, no character rim light.
- **Prototype surfaces remain:** flat grey quays, repeated facades, plain boxes (Walt P1s; the harbour
  flotsam was removed in #175).

three.js 0.186 already ships the post nodes we're missing (`examples/jsm/tsl/display/`): `GTAONode`,
`SSAONode`, `BloomNode`, `TRAANode`, `TAAUNode`, `SMAANode`, `DepthOfFieldNode`, `Lut3DNode`, `OutlineNode`,
`GodraysNode`, `LensflareNode`, `MotionBlur`. The limit is pipeline and frame budgets (PLAN §12.1, §15.4),
not missing code.

## 3. What Walt would ask for, in order of "wow per hour"

1. **Light the show.** Real bloom, half-res GTAO, a per-island colour-grading LUT that follows the time of
   day, warm rim light on characters, and depth of field in conversations. High/Ultra first, with Low and
   Lite unchanged (the UHD 620 floor).
2. **Bring faces to life.** Blinks, eye darts, gaze at whoever's talking, brow beats on key words, and lip
   sync on every voiced line. This lifts every character we already have, today.
3. **Make the cast distinct.** Each teacher gets their own silhouette (proportions by bone scale), costume
   pieces and a signature prop (Walt's list: Mina's map baton, Rhea's helm compass, Oren's AI gauge, the
   Admiral's seal ledger, Tobias's crate manifest).
4. **Real acting.** Replace the sine gestures with mocap gesture clips, layered with breathing, weight
   shifts and reactions to quest beats.
5. **Real surfaces where the camera lands.** Scanned props and textures (weathered planks, rope, nets,
   lanterns, crates, cobbles, plaster, roof tiles) at every arrival and conversation spot, plus decals and
   wear. No flat-coloured primitive in a hero frame.
6. **New hero characters** in one consistent "stylized realism" look (exaggerated, appealing proportions,
   rich materials, expressive faces), once 1–5 have proved the pipeline.

## 4. How to do each one

### 4.1 Lighting and post

- Add `BloomNode` (threshold on HDR, not the current LDR lift), `GTAONode` at half resolution, and
  `Lut3DNode` with one LUT per island × time of day. Revisit `SMAANode` or `TRAANode` (TRAA needs ocean
  velocity, #12) for the thin rigging that aliases today.
- Conversation shots: `DepthOfFieldNode` on the speaker, plus a two-light character rig (warm key from the
  sun side, cool rim behind). The cameras already exist (`talkShot`).
- Keep each addition behind a quality flag and inside the pipeline count. Measure with `npm run bench` and
  the hitch guard before and after.

### 4.2 Faces and lip sync (works on today's characters)

- **Viseme data at build time.** `tools/voice/generate.ts` already calls Azure Speech. Turn on viseme
  events and save each line's viseme ids and offsets next to its audio. Viseme ids look supported for our
  en-GB neural voices, but confirm with one line first. Full 3D blend shapes (55 ARKit-style shapes) are
  en-US and zh-CN only. For any voice that returns no visemes (check Wingman's DragonHD voice), run
  [Rhubarb Lip Sync](https://github.com/DanielSWolf/rhubarb-lip-sync) (MIT) on the audio and transcript.
- **A face rig that suits stylized characters:** eyes and mouth as an atlas of drawn shapes (mouth per
  viseme group, eyes open/half/closed plus a few moods) on a face decal or the head UVs, plus a jaw bone if
  the mesh has one. This is cheap, reads well at game distance, and is how many appealing stylized games
  do it. Blinks every 2–6 s, double blinks, eye darts, and gaze that follows the speaker.

### 4.3 Acting

- Mocap gesture clips: Quaternius Universal Animation Library emotes (CC0, already in our pipeline) and
  Rocketbox's talk/gesture clips (MIT), retargeted with a port of Tidewater's world-space Blender retarget
  (`tools/characters/convert.py`, MIT; keep its header and add `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`).
- Tag key words in each script line ("point", "count", "proud", "worried") so the gesture and brow beats
  land on the right word, using the subtitle alignment we already have.

### 4.4 New hero characters (after the slice proves the bar)

| Route | Quality | Licence | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Commission a character artist** (sculpt, retopo, rig, face shapes) | Best and most consistent | Work-for-hire | The surest way to Disney-level appeal for the ~12 teachers and 2 captains. |
| AI-assisted: concept art from our image model, then image-to-3D, Blender cleanup, rig to the UAL skeleton | Good bodies; faces and topology need cleanup | Meshy / Tripo: paid plans for commercial rights (free tiers differ). TRELLIS 2: MIT, but needs a CUDA GPU (not the M3 Max) | Log every output in `ART_PROVENANCE.md`. Use one style guide and prompt template for consistency. |
| Rocketbox avatars, as Tidewater does | Good, realistic | MIT | Modern clothes, no pirates, realistic style. Useful for its mocap clips, not for the cast. |
| Hunyuan3D | Good | Tencent community licence **does not apply in the EU, UK or South Korea** | Don't use for a public web game. |

Rebuild **Wingman** as a proper rigged parrot (wing, tail, head and beak bones, blinks), either authored or
procedural in Tidewater's `BirdShapes`/`BirdPose` style. He's the guide, so he's a hero character.

### 4.5 Surfaces and set dressing

- Adapt Tidewater's `tools/props/` pipeline: Poly Haven CC0 models and textures, decimated into LODs,
  1024² KTX2, meshopt. Log each one in `ASSETS.md`.
- Start where the camera lands: Anchorage's pier and quay, Meridian's arrival quay and plaza, and each
  teacher's conversation spot. Paving, kerbs, steps, wear, puddle decals, ropes, nets, lanterns, stalls.

### 4.6 One style

Write a one-page art bible before new characters: shape language, head-to-body ratio, palette per island,
material rules (painted-looking PBR, no flat fills), and reference boards (technique only, no copied
assets). A good target is "stylized realism", like a pirate game with real water and exaggerated,
appealing people. Every feature then passes a **Walt gate**: fixed-camera screenshots and contact sheets
(`tools/characters/npc-contact-sheet.mjs`) checked for silhouette, faces, staging, no prototype surfaces
and no clipping.

## 5. Proposed order

1. **Vertical slice:** Anchorage pier at golden hour with Nell. Post upgrades
   (bloom, GTAO, LUT, conversation DOF and rim light), Nell's face rig with lip sync on her lines, two
   mocap gestures, her signature prop, and scanned props on the pier. Before/after shots and bench numbers
   for every tier.
2. If it passes the Walt gate: roll faces, lip sync and gestures out to every voiced character, and give
   each teacher a distinct silhouette and prop.
3. Surfaces and set dressing at every arrival and conversation spot (this joins the "more life" step).
4. Wingman's rebuild.
5. New hero characters by the chosen route in §4.4, starting with the two captains.

## 6. Risks

- **Budgets:** bloom and SMAA were cut once for pipeline counts (#40). Measure each addition and keep
  Low/Lite unchanged.
- **Download size:** scanned props and new characters add megabytes. Use KTX2, meshopt and per-island packs
  (PLAN §13).
- **Consistency:** mixing sources (Quaternius, scans, AI) is what split the style before. The art bible
  and Walt gate come first.
- **Licences:** MIT code keeps its notice (`THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`), CC0 and MIT assets go in
  `ASSETS.md`, AI outputs in `ART_PROVENANCE.md`. No Octocat or Copilot logo, ever.
